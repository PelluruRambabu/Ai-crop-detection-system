# Ai-crop-detection-system
AI crop detection uses computer vision to analyze leaf images in real time, automatically identifying plant species, diagnosing diseases, and suggesting treatments to protect yields.
AI Crop Disease Detection and Pathology Diagnostic System An end-to-end computer vision and deep learning framework developed to identify, classify, and diagnose foliar plant diseases across major agricultural crops. The system integrates deep convolutional neural networks with high-resolution optical capture to deliver real-time diagnostic reporting, disease severity profiling, and actionable agronomic treatments directly from leaf imagery.Executive SummaryCrop foliar diseases caused by fungi, bacteria, viruses, and insect pests pose a severe threat to global food security and farming profitability. Early, localized detection is critical to arrest pathogen transmission before widespread field defoliation occurs. Conventional inspection methodologies rely heavily on manual human scouting, which is labor-intensive, subjective, and prone to misdiagnosis during early infection phases.The AI Crop Disease Detection System provides an automated diagnostic tool that analyses leaf morphology using modern transfer-learning architectures. Operating directly on visual inputs from mobile uploads, webcams, or field camera streams, the system categorizes leaves into healthy tissue or specific pathogen-induced conditions. Each inference yields a disease classification, a statistical confidence score, a pathogen severity assessment, and targeted chemical or biological treatment plans to minimize crop losses.Targeted Crop Species and PathologiesThe diagnostic model is trained to recognize pathologies across three primary staple crops: Tomato, Maize (Corn), and Rice (Paddy).   Tomato PathologiesTomato Healthy: Optimal vegetative foliar structure without necrotic lesions, chlorosis, or curling.Tomato Leaf Blight: Fungal infection (Alternaria solani / Phytophthora infestans) characterized by dark, concentric target-like rings and leaf desiccation.   Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV): Severe viral affliction transmitted by whiteflies (Bemisia tabaci), resulting in upward leaf curling, vein clearing, and stunting.   Tomato Septoria Leaf Spot: Foliar fungal pathogen (Septoria lycopersici) causing small, circular spots with dark brown margins and grey centers.   Tomato Verticillium Wilt: Vascular fungal pathogen (Verticillium dahliae) causing characteristic V-shaped chlorotic lesions along leaf margins.   Maize (Corn) PathologiesMaize Healthy: Vigorous leaf blades with clean venation and absence of foliar feeding damage.   Maize Fall Armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda): Destructive pest infestation leading to ragged foliar margins, window-pane feeding patterns, and whorl destruction.   Maize Grasshopper Damage: Irregular defoliation and margin chewing caused by Acrididae grasshoppers and locusts.   Maize Leaf Beetle Damage: Pinhole perforations and skeletonized feeding marks resulting from Chrysomelidae beetle activity.   Northern Corn Leaf Blight: Large, elliptical, cigar-shaped necrotic lesions caused by the fungus Exserohilum turcicum.   Maize Leaf Spot (Grey Leaf Spot): Rectangular, blocky lesions confined by parallel leaf veins caused by Cercospora zeae-maydis.   Maize Streak Virus: Viral disease transmitted by Cicadulina leafhoppers, displaying narrow, broken chlorotic stripes along the leaf blade.   Rice (Paddy) PathologiesRice Healthy: Uniform green leaf blades free of lesions or necrotic tip dieback.Rice Bacterial Blight: Devastating vascular bacterial disease caused by Xanthomonas oryzae, producing water-soaked to yellowish-white lesions with wavy margins progressing from leaf tips.Rice Blast: Severe fungal disease caused by Magnaporthe oryzae, identified by spindle-shaped, diamond-like lesions with whitish-grey centers and reddish-brown borders.Rice Brown Spot: Fungal infection caused by Bipolaris oryzae, characterized by round to oval brown lesions distributed across the leaf surface.Machine Learning Pipeline and Neural ArchitectureThe image classification engine is built upon the MobileNetV2 deep convolutional neural network, selected for its parameter efficiency, low latency, and high feature extraction performance.Data Preprocessing and HarmonizationInput datasets sourced from standard image repositories and YOLO/Roboflow annotation exports are systematically harmonized into clean directory structures. Images are converted into uniform three-channel RGB formats, scaled, and normalized using standard ImageNet distribution constants ($\mu = [0.485, 0.456, 0.406]$ and $\sigma = [0.229, 0.224, 0.225]$).Data Augmentation StrategyTo maximize generalizability and prevent overfitting under variable natural lighting conditions, training batches undergo dynamic spatial and color transformations:Random horizontal flipping with a 50% probability to account for leaf orientation variance.Minor random rotations ($\pm 15^\circ$) to simulate realistic camera positioning angles.Color jittering on brightness and contrast to handle indoor artificial light and outdoor sunlight glare.Transfer Learning StrategyThe convolutional base of MobileNetV2 uses pretrained ImageNet feature extractors. The terminal classification head is customized by replacing the final linear layer with an output dimension corresponding to the targeted crop pathology classes. The network is trained using the Adam Optimizer and Cross-Entropy Loss, evaluated across stratified training and validation sets.Model Export ArtifactThe trained weights, classification indices, and model metadata are bundled into a single serialized payload: crop_disease_model.pkl. This design eliminates external dependency on separate scaler files, allowing direct deployment in web environments.Interactive Web Dashboard and Diagnostic EngineThe user-facing interface is constructed with a dark cyber-HUD visual aesthetic, providing an intuitive, interactive environment for field personnel and researchers.   Multi-Source Optical IngestionThe diagnostic interface supports three flexible image acquisition channels:ESP32-CAM MJPEG Video Stream: Connects over the local network via IP configuration to preview live camera feeds with targeting crosshairs.   Laptop / USB Camera Feed: Captures snapshots directly from connected USB optical webcams for laboratory examination.Static File Uploader: Accepts manual image uploads (.jpg, .jpeg, .png) for offline testing and historical validation.Real-Time Inference and Treatment ProfilingWhen an optical scan is initiated, the system passes the normalized tensor through the neural network. The dashboard displays:Target Classification: The precise species and diagnosed disease condition.   Confidence Rating: The mathematical probability match computed via Softmax activation.   Severity Index: An operational alert level categorized from None to Critical.Agronomic Recommendation: Actionable guidance including recommended chemical active ingredients, biological vector control, and cultural hygiene practices.ML Dataset Framework and CSV ExportEvery completed scan appends an entry to an in-memory session log. The table displays chronological timestamps, crop identities, diagnosed pathologies, confidence percentages, and severity rankings. A single-click Download CSV feature allows users to export all historical diagnostic logs into a structured spreadsheet for record-keeping and field analytics.
